Politicians.social is a plan for a mission-driven social enterprise that helps restore trust in
political discussion online. It uses the Fediverse to give federal politicians, journalists and
citizens a secure, transparent and decentralised place to talk, without profit-driven algorithms or
billionaire-controlled moderation getting in the way.

Most platforms outsource their architecture to global monopolies. Politicians.social is committed to
**sovereign technical capability** instead: Australian engineers building on open-source foundations
(Linux and FOSS), so the infrastructure of our democracy is owned, operated and understood by
Australians.

## What the Fediverse is, and why it matters

The Fediverse is a global, decentralised network of independently run social media servers that talk
to each other using open protocols like ActivityPub. On X or Facebook, one company owns the hardware,
the data and the rules. The Fediverse works more like email: you pick a provider (an "instance") that
shares your values, and you can still follow and talk with people on entirely different servers.

That removes central points of failure and corporate monopolies. No single company can silence voices
or steer public opinion with opaque algorithms, and people keep more control over their digital
identity and what they see. It's the technical foundation for a public square that's resilient,
interoperable and community-governed.

## Why politicians should join

For elected officials, today's social media is volatile and unreliable. A dedicated Fediverse server
gives them back ownership of their online presence. They can talk directly with constituents in a
high-signal space, free of bot-driven harassment and commercial surveillance, where the focus is policy
and public service rather than engagement metrics that reward division.

## Why a not-for-profit social enterprise

A not-for-profit social enterprise is the only structure that ties the platform's survival to the
public interest. Without pressure to maximise shareholder value, it can put privacy, ethical moderation
and long-term stability first. Revenue from transparent subscriptions or institutional grants goes back
into infrastructure and safety, so the platform stays a public utility rather than a data-mining
operation. It also grows Australian jobs and technical expertise here at home.

## A phased roll-out, with verification

To keep integrity high, people join in tiers:

1. **Verified federal politicians**, a core of authenticated, high-trust accounts that anchor the
   platform's credibility (`au.politicians.social`).
2. **Accredited journalists**, bringing in the press for factual reporting and real-time accountability
   (`journo.host.au`).
3. **State and local officials**, connecting national policy and local government
   (`vic.au.politicians.social`, `nsw.au.politicians.social`).
4. **The general public**, through existing servers such as `aus.social`, local community servers like
   `locals.portphillip.vic.aus.social`, and organisation servers like `nedfoundation.org.au` for small
   groups that want verification on the Fediverse.

## Moderation you control, with the whole network's reach

A dedicated, professional moderation team that understands political speech and safety is one of the
biggest advantages. Local community standards stay strict, to prevent abuse and misinformation, yet
members still get the network effect of the wider Fediverse: a curated safe haven that isn't cut off
from the global conversation.

## Structure: a company limited by guarantee

To operate Australia-wide, the right structure is a **company limited by guarantee (CLG)**. It's a
national entity regulated by ASIC and the ACNC, so there's no need to register in every state, and it's
the structure serious grant-makers and government bodies expect from a professional social enterprise.

It has a board of directors. In keeping with democratic and Mastodon values, the constitution would have
directors elected by the members, such as the journalists and civil society members on the platform.

## A mission lock in the constitution

To keep the organisation true to Doughnut Economics, open source and democracy, those principles get
written into the constitution:

- **Objects clause:** the company exists to "advance the security and integrity of Australia's
  democratic discourse through the provision of open-source, decentralised digital infrastructure."
- **Doughnut clause:** a social and environmental responsibility clause requiring the board to consider
  the ecological ceiling (such as carbon-neutral hosting) and the social foundation (such as
  accessibility and safety) in every procurement and operational decision.
- **Asset lock:** if the company ever closes, all assets (code, data and funds) must go to another
  not-for-profit with similar open-source and democratic goals. That rules out a Twitter-style private
  buyout.

## The road map

1. **Draft the constitution** with the mission lock and not-for-profit clauses, meeting ACNC
   requirements for a charity.
2. **Incorporate with ASIC** as a company limited by guarantee (at least three directors and a
   secretary).
3. **Register with the ACNC** as a charity, under "advancing security or safety of Australia" or
   "advancing education" (digital literacy and democracy).
4. **Apply for tax concessions:** once registered, the ATO grants income tax exemption and GST
   concessions.
5. **Seek DGR endorsement**, the last and hardest step, likely with a specialist not-for-profit lawyer
   framing the harm-prevention or educational work to meet the ATO's criteria.

## Draft objects

The company would be a non-partisan social enterprise operating for the public benefit, with these
objects:

**Advance democratic integrity and public discourse.** Provide secure, transparent, decentralised
infrastructure for civic communication in Australia; foster a healthy online public square that values
facts, accountability and respectful engagement between elected officials and the public; and protect
democratic institutions by reducing coordinated inauthentic behaviour, misinformation and algorithmic
manipulation.

**Steward federated, open-source infrastructure.** Run a social media server on open protocols such as
ActivityPub that works with the global Fediverse; support open-source software and open standards that
empower users and prevent corporate monopolies over public data; and ensure data sovereignty for
Australian users through transparent data management and privacy by design.

**High-trust verification and anti-impersonation.** Run a multi-tier verification framework,
prioritising federal representatives and candidates, then accredited journalists and public-interest
media, then state and local officials, then civil society organisations and community leaders, and give
the public reliable ways to tell official accounts from unverified or parody ones.

**Promote public-interest journalism and civic institutions.** Support a free press with a platform
for verified news where journalists can engage safely with the public and officials, and work with
civic institutions on digital literacy and understanding of democratic processes.

**Operate on regenerative economic principles.** Apply Doughnut Economics: a strong social foundation
(safety, inclusion and voice) within ecological ceilings (carbon-neutral hosting and ethical
procurement), with every surplus reinvested in the mission, infrastructure and moderation rather than
paid to shareholders.

**Independent, accountable moderation.** Human-led moderation, independent of government and political
parties, balancing local control with the benefits of the global Fediverse.

**Build Australian technical capability.** Recruit, train and employ Australian-based engineers to
build and run the core infrastructure, so the keys to the kingdom, and the know-how to use them, stay in
Australian hands. Contribute improvements, security fixes and new work back to the global open-source
commons, and partner with Australian educators and community groups to train the next generation of
civic technologists in Linux, networking and systems architecture in the service of democracy.
