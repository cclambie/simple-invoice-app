This is the briefing note I've been sending to ministers and their offices. It's short on purpose:
the case for the Fediverse as public communication infrastructure, on two pages.

> **Summary:** Australia's public communication increasingly depends on foreign-owned digital
> platforms whose incentives are commercial rather than civic. The Fediverse is a practical,
> open-standard alternative that can strengthen national sovereignty, improve resilience, support
> journalism, and restore more direct communication between institutions and citizens.

**The current risk: platform dependency.** Government communication and public discussion rely on a
small number of private platforms. They can change the rules, visibility and access at any time,
outside Australian control.

**The opportunity: federated sovereignty.** A federated model lets trusted institutions run their own
servers and verified identities while staying connected through open standards. That's resilience
without isolation.

## 1. The problem: strategic vulnerability

Australia's digital public square is effectively rented from global technology platforms. That creates
structural risks for government, media and democracy:

- **Sovereignty:** public communication depends on infrastructure owned and governed offshore.
- **Information integrity:** algorithms often reward outrage, distortion and low-trust engagement over
  authoritative information.
- **Data security:** public interactions pass through commercial systems with limited transparency and
  patchy accountability.

## 2. The solution: the Fediverse model

Australia doesn't need to depend entirely on commercial platforms to talk to the public. It can build
and support a sovereign public communication layer on open standards.

The Fediverse isn't a single platform. It's a network of interoperable services built on open
protocols such as ActivityPub. Like email, each organisation can run its own service and still connect
to everyone else. Government departments, public institutions, journalists and media organisations
keep control of identity, moderation and hosting while staying discoverable and connected.

## 3. Why it matters for government and journalism

- **Direct public communication:** official voices reach citizens without handing visibility to opaque
  algorithms.
- **Resilience:** communication keeps working even if a commercial platform changes policy, loses trust
  or becomes unavailable.
- **Support for journalism:** journalists and publishers can rebuild direct relationships with their
  audiences instead of relying on engagement-driven middlemen.
- **Open competition:** standards reduce lock-in and make room for innovation by public institutions,
  media organisations and Australian technology providers.

## 4. A practical first step

A phased federal approach could start with a pilot of verified institutional and public-interest
accounts, with journalists and political figures taking part. That would let government test
governance, verification and public engagement in a low-risk, standards-based setting.

## 5. An invitation: au.politicians.social

[au.politicians.social](https://au.politicians.social) is a verified-accounts-only Fediverse server for
political representatives and public figures. It's a practical starting point for elected
representatives, candidates, senior public figures and trusted institutions to build a credible
federated presence in a controlled, clearly verified environment.

Joining is an immediate way to:

- establish an authentic public voice on the Fediverse
- take part in a trusted Australian civic network
- show leadership on digital sovereignty and democratic communication
- help shape the norms of a healthier public communication system

## Recommendation

The Australian Government should treat federated social infrastructure as a strategic policy
opportunity: one that supports sovereignty, democratic resilience, and a stronger relationship between
public institutions, journalism and citizens.

An early, visible step would be to encourage participation in trusted Australian Fediverse spaces such
as au.politicians.social, while exploring a broader framework for public-interest federated
infrastructure.
